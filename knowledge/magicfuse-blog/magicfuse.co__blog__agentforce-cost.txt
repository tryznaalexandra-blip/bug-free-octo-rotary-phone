Title: Agentforce Cost in 2026: What You Need to Know
Fetched at (unix): 1777287291

Agentforce pricing has changed significantly since its initial launch. The original model, a flat $2 per conversation, is no longer the only payment option. In May 2025, Salesforce introduced Flex Credits, a consumption-based system where businesses pay per action rather than per conversation. Since then, Salesforce has added per-user licensing, employee-facing add-ons, bundled editions, and multiple payment models. As of 2026, the Agentforce pricing page shows that Salesforce now offers multiple pricing models for Agentforce.

That makes cost planning harder than it used to be, but also more flexible. The right pricing model depends on whether you're deploying customer-facing agents, employee-facing agents, or both; how many actions your agents will perform; and how predictable your usage is.

This guide breaks down every Agentforce pricing option available in 2026, explains which model fits which use case, covers implementation costs not listed on Salesforce's pricing page, and shows how to estimate your total investment before you commit. Whether you're exploring Agentforce for the first time through Salesforce Foundations or scaling an existing deployment across departments, this is the pricing information you need to make an informed decision.

Agentforce Pricing in 2026 at a Glance

Salesforce currently offers six pricing paths for Agentforce. The right one depends on your use case, team size, and whether your agents are customer-facing, employee-facing, or both. Here's the full picture based on the official Agentforce pricing page:

Salesforce Foundations (Free Tier) — $0

Available to all existing Salesforce customers on Enterprise Edition or above. Includes Agent Builder, Prompt Builder, 200,000 Flex Credits, and 250,000 Data Cloud credits. Designed for testing use cases and running proof-of-concept pilots before committing to a paid plan. Credits are consumed as agents perform actions, and overage fees apply once the free allocation runs out. Usage can be tracked in Digital Wallet.

Flex Credits — $500 per 100,000 credits

This is Salesforce's recommended consumption-based model for most new deployments. Each standard action an agent performs (updating a record, answering a question from a knowledge base, summarizing a case) consumes 20 Flex Credits, which works out to roughly $0.10 per action. Voice actions consume 30 Flex Credits ($0.15 per action). Credit consumption is tracked in real time through Digital Wallet. See the Agentforce Rate Card for a full breakdown of credit usage by action type.

Conversations — $2 per conversation

The original Agentforce pricing model, still available for organizations that prefer flat per-conversation billing. Best suited for external customer-oriented agents where per-interaction pricing is simpler to forecast. Note that conversation pricing is available through pre-purchase only — pay-as-you-go and pre-commit options are not available for this model.

Agentforce User License — $5 per user per month (requires Flex Credits)

A lightweight license that gives every employee access to Agentforce agents, with metered usage drawing from your Flex Credit pool. This is designed for company-wide rollouts where you want broad access but want to control costs through consumption-based billing.

Agentforce Add-Ons — $125 per user per month for Sales, Service, and Field Service; $150 per user per month for Industries Clouds

These add-ons provide unmetered Agentforce usage for licensed employees, along with Salesforce's full suite of predictive, generative, and agentic AI, Prompt Builder, and AI-powered analytics. This is the option for teams where employees will use Agentforce heavily enough that consumption-based billing would exceed the flat per-user fee.

Agentforce 1 Editions — from $550 per user per month

The most comprehensive package, bundling the Agentforce add-on with 1 million Flex Credits per org per year and 2.5 million Data 360 Credits per org per year. Built for enterprises that need extensive AI access across multiple departments and want predictable annual budgeting.

One critical rule to keep in mind: Flex Credits and conversation pricing cannot be used in the same Salesforce org. You must choose one consumption model per organization. If you're currently on conversation pricing and want to switch to Flex Credits, you'll need to swap all existing Agentforce conversation SKUs - talk to your Salesforce Account Executive to manage the transition.

Flex Credits vs. Conversations vs. User Licenses

Choosing between Agentforce's pricing models isn't just a billing decision; it determines how flexibly you can scale, how granularly you can track costs, and how predictable your monthly spend will be. Here's how each model works in practice and when it makes sense.

When Flex Credits Are the Better Fit

Flex Credits are Salesforce's default recommendation for new Agentforce deployments, and for good reason. Because you pay per action rather than per conversation, your costs align directly with what your agents actually do. A simple interaction that triggers one action costs $0.10. A complex conversation that involves six actions costs $0.60. You're never overpaying for lightweight interactions or underestimating the cost of complex ones.

Flex Credits also cover both customer-oriented and employee-facing agents, which makes them the most versatile option if you plan to deploy Agentforce across departments. A single pool of credits can serve your service team's customer support agent, your sales team's coaching agent, and your HR team's onboarding assistant, all tracked in one place through Digital Wallet.

Choose Flex Credits when your agents handle a mix of simple and complex interactions, when you're deploying across multiple teams or use cases, when you want granular visibility into what each agent and action costs, or when your usage volume is variable and hard to predict month to month.

To put the math in context: Salesforce's own pricing page illustrates a case management scenario where 100 users each handle 3 cases per day across 20 working days. Each case triggers 3 actions (60 Flex Credits), totaling 360,000 credits per month, or $1,800.

When Conversation Pricing Makes More Sense

Conversation pricing charges a flat $2 per conversation regardless of how many actions happen within it. That simplicity is its main advantage. If your average conversation involves more than 20 actions (since 20 actions × $0.10 = $2.00 in Flex Credits), conversation pricing becomes the cheaper option. However, real costs depend on action types, token usage, and orchestration complexity. For high-touch customer support scenarios where agents perform many steps per interaction, identifying the customer, pulling case history, checking entitlements, updating records, and sending follow-ups, the flat rate can save money.

However, conversation pricing is limited in two important ways. First, it's optimized for external customer-oriented agents only. If you also need employee-facing agents, you'll need a separate pricing path for those. Second, it's available only through pre-purchase — you commit to a set volume upfront. Pay-as-you-go and pre-commit billing options are exclusive to Flex Credits.

Choose conversation pricing when you're running a straightforward customer support or chat deployment, when your conversations are consistently action-heavy (20+ actions each), and when you prefer the simplicity of a single per-interaction cost without tracking individual actions.

When Per-User Licensing Is Worth the Premium

The Agentforce User License at $5 per user per month is not a standalone option - it requires Flex Credits and simply unlocks Agentforce access for employees who don't already have it through a Sales, Service, or other Cloud license. Think of it as the access layer, while Flex Credits remain the billing layer for actual usage.

The add-ons at $125–$150 per user per month are a different calculation entirely. These provide unmetered Agentforce usage for each licensed employee. The break-even logic is straightforward: if a single employee's monthly Agentforce consumption would exceed $125 worth of Flex Credits, the add-on saves money. In practice, the add-on makes sense well before that theoretical ceiling — the predictability and the inclusion of Salesforce's full AI suite (predictive, generative, and agentic AI plus analytics) add value beyond pure credit math.

Choose per-user add-ons when your employees use Agentforce heavily throughout the day, when budget predictability matters more than granular usage optimization, or when you need the full AI suite bundled into one monthly cost per seat.

The Agentforce 1 Editions at $550+ per user per month make sense for enterprises that want the add-on plus a built-in annual allocation of Flex Credits (1 million per org per year) and Data 360 Credits (2.5 million per org per year); essentially a single bundled SKU that covers both employee and customer-facing needs.

A Quick Decision Framework

For teams still weighing options, it comes down to three questions.
Who are your agents serving: customers, employees, or both? If both, Flex Credits give you the broadest coverage from a single model.

How complex are your interactions? If conversations are consistently action-heavy, flat conversation pricing may be cheaper.

And how important is cost predictability? If you need a fixed monthly cost per employee, the add-ons or editions provide that, while Flex Credits offer more flexibility but variable spend.

Agentforce Payment Models

Beyond choosing between Flex Credits and Conversations, you also need to decide how you want to pay. Salesforce introduced three distinct billing models in 2025 to give buyers more control over cash flow and commitment levels. Each is designed for a different financial posture. These details come from Salesforce's payment options announcement and the pricing page.

Pre-purchase works like buying credits in bulk upfront for the full length of your contract. You pay once, and usage draws down from your balance over time. This is the model that offers the deepest discounts, making it the best fit for organizations with predictable, consistent Agentforce usage who can confidently forecast their annual credit consumption. Pre-purchase is also the only billing model available for conversation-based pricing, if you choose the $2-per-conversation path, this is how you'll pay for it.

Pay-as-you-go removes the upfront commitment entirely. You start using Agentforce and get billed monthly in arrears for the Flex Credits you actually consumed. Costs scale directly with your needs, so you never pay for credits you don't use. This is the lowest-risk option for organizations that are just getting started with Agentforce, running initial pilots, or scaling gradually. The trade-off is that you won't get the volume discounts available with pre-purchase or pre-commit.

Pre-commit sits between the other two. You commit to a baseline level of Agentforce usage upfront (the bigger the commitment, the better the pricing) but you don't pay upfront. Instead, you're billed monthly in arrears based on actual usage. If your usage falls below your commitment at the end of the term, a true-up charge covers the difference. This model suits organizations that have a reasonable usage forecast and want favorable pricing without tying up cash. According to the pricing page, pre-commit is being made widely available through 2026.

A few things to note. Only Flex Credits are available across all three payment models. Conversation pricing, Agentforce add-ons ($125–$150/user/month), and Agentforce 1 Editions ($550+/user/month) are not available through pay-as-you-go or pre-commit; they are purchased through standard Salesforce contracting.

Also, unused Flex Credits do not roll over into subsequent subscription terms regardless of payment model, so it's important to right-size your purchase. Salesforce's Digital Wallet provides near-real-time usage tracking, proactive threshold alerts, and trend analysis to help you monitor consumption and avoid both overages and waste.

What Affects Your Total Agentforce Cost

The prices on Salesforce's pricing page (Flex Credits, conversations, licenses) are only part of the picture. Your actual Agentforce investment depends on several additional factors that influence how quickly credits are consumed and how much effort is required to get agents production-ready.

Volume and Complexity of Agent Actions

This is the most direct cost driver. A customer support agent that answers simple knowledge-base questions consumes fewer credits per interaction than a field service agent that identifies a customer, looks up work types, checks appointment availability, and schedules a visit. Salesforce's pricing page illustrates this clearly: a case management use case with 3 actions per case costs 60 Flex Credits ($0.30), while a field service scheduling use case with 6 actions costs 120 Flex Credits ($0.60). Over thousands of interactions per month, that difference compounds.

Voice vs. Text

If you plan to deploy Agentforce Voice for phone-based interactions, know that voice actions consume 30 Flex Credits each compared to 20 for standard text-based actions — a 50% premium. For organizations with high call volumes, voice pricing can meaningfully shift your monthly spend.

Token Thresholds

Actions that process large amounts of data can cost more than the standard 20 credits. If a single action exceeds 10,000 tokens (for example, loading extensive customer history into context), it counts as multiple actions. An action processing 20,001 tokens would consume 60 credits instead of 20. Optimizing agent prompts and limiting the amount of context injected into each action is one of the most effective ways to control credit consumption. The Flex Credits Rate Card has the full details on multipliers.

Number of Users and Agent Types

Costs scale with both the number of people using Agentforce and the number of agent types deployed. Adding a Sales Coach agent alongside an existing Service Agent doesn't just mean more conversations; it means a broader surface area of credit consumption across different workflows.

Customer-Facing vs. Employee-Facing Deployment

customer oriented agents tend to generate higher volumes of interactions but may have simpler per-interaction action counts. Employee agents may handle fewer interactions but with greater complexity per session. The mix affects whether consumption pricing or per-user licensing makes more financial sense.

Data Cloud and Data 360 Usage

Agentforce agents that leverage Data Cloud for real-time customer insights, segmentation, or unstructured data analysis consume Data 360 Credits in addition to Flex Credits. These are a separate credit pool tracked alongside Agentforce consumption in Digital Wallet. The free allocation with Salesforce Foundations includes 250,000 Data Cloud credits, but production workloads that rely heavily on data unification and real-time processing may incur additional charges.

Sandbox and Testing Usage

Running Agentforce in sandbox environments does consume credits, though at a reduced rate. According to the Flex Credits Rate Card, sandbox actions draw credits at roughly 80% of the production rate (16 credits per standard action vs. 20 in production). This is more cost-efficient than production testing, but it's not free, and organizations that run extensive QA cycles should factor sandbox consumption into their budget.

Costs Beyond Agentforce Licensing

Salesforce's pricing page tells you what Agentforce costs to run. It doesn't tell you what it costs to build, launch, and maintain. For most organizations, these non-license costs represent a significant share of the total investment.

Customization and Development

Out-of-the-box agents handle standard workflows, but most businesses need agents tailored to their processes and data. Building custom topics, writing instructions, and connecting agents to existing Flows, Apex, or MuleSoft APIs takes real development effort. Agentforce 3 introduced over 200 prebuilt industry actions that can reduce build time, but the more specialized your workflows, the higher the cost.

Integration

Connecting Agentforce to third-party systems (ERP, accounting, proprietary databases) may require middleware, MuleSoft configuration, or custom API work. Salesforce supports API-based and event-driven integrations across systems, but complex enterprise integrations still need dedicated planning and resources.

Data Preparation

Agents are only as good as the data they access. Knowledge articles need to be accurate, customer records need to be clean, and documentation needs to be structured for retrieval. Organizations migrating data into Salesforce or Data Cloud for the first time should expect a significant effort in extraction, cleansing, and transformation.

Testing and QA

AI agents are non-deterministic; they can respond differently to the same input depending on context. Salesforce's Agentforce Testing Center lets teams auto-generate synthetic test interactions and validate agent behavior at scale in sandbox environments. The tool is included with Agentforce, but the time required to design test scenarios, analyze results, and iterate on instructions across multiple cycles is a real cost to budget for.

Training

Agent Builder and Prompt Builder are low-code, but your team still needs to learn how to configure agents effectively, write clear instructions, interpret Digital Wallet data, and manage escalations. For teams without in-house Salesforce AI expertise, structured onboarding through a partner or Salesforce Professional Services may be necessary.

Ongoing Governance

Agentforce isn't set-and-forget. Agents need monitoring to ensure accuracy, guardrail compliance, and ROI. Digital Wallet tracks usage, the Command Center (introduced in Agentforce 3) provides real-time health metrics and conversation replay, and Agentforce Analytics measures adoption. Someone on your team needs to own this ongoing oversight.

Platform Prerequisites

Agentforce requires Salesforce Enterprise Edition or above. If you're not already on that tier, the base platform cost is a dependency that directly affects your total investment.
