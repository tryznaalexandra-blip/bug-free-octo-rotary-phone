Part of Salesforce delivery risk can sit in contract language that reads as routine on paper.

IBM has reported that defects found after release can cost far more to fix than issues caught during requirements and design. Actual ratios depend on the study and context. In Salesforce programs, similar dynamics can show up when SOW language stays non-specific. (Add primary source link if required.)

A few contract themes where unclear wording often leads to follow-up questions:

“Configuration” without metadata boundaries

If “configuration” is not tied to explicit technical limits, scope can expand in ways that are hard to track later.
Net-new Apex/LWC, managed package installs, custom integrations, Einstein/Data Cloud enablement, and automation redesign are examples of work that can change implementation effort when they are not listed up front.

Missing ALM and environment ownership

Sandbox strategy, branching model, deployment approvals, merge ownership, and integration-test seeding are sometimes left at a high level in the baseline — and then become coordination work during release windows.

Training and hypercare treated as placeholders

Which business personas are trained? Through which channels? For how long after cutover?
When those items stay generic, adoption friction can be reported later as “system instability” even when the underlying issue is training or process.

Integrations reduced to a single line item

Real-time vs batch processing, API vs file exchange, retry logic, reconciliation handling, external IDs, dependency sequencing, and rollback behavior are examples of details that affect production behavior and are easier to align on when they are written down early.

Clear statements of work tend to list deliverables and also spell out technical assumptions, ownership, and basic production-readiness expectations while there is still room to adjust.

That kind of clarity supports delivery planning. It is separate from legal review and from any commitment by MagicFuse unless agreed in a signed agreement.
