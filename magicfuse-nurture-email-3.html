One reason Salesforce projects drift is that the partner scope looks clear commercially, but stays vague technically.

McKinsey found that large IT projects run 45% over budget on average and deliver 56% less value than expected — usually because assumptions surface too late. ()

Before signing with an SI, three questions are worth pressure-testing:

1

What assumptions are included in the estimate?

Existing Flow/Apex debt, managed packages, integration complexity, data migration, sandbox availability, and UAT coverage.
2

How will scope changes be handled?

What happens when new fields, flows, reports, integrations, or approval logic appear after the baseline?
3

What does “done” mean in production?

Not “built in sandbox,” but deployed, tested, UAT-signed, monitored, and ready for rollback if integrations fail.

A useful rule: don’t only compare SI estimates by price — compare the assumptions, exclusions, and acceptance criteria behind them.
