# Lost SQL nurture strategy

## Goal

Re-engage lost SQLs with the simplest topics that can fit an already active campaign, without needing a heavy content build.

## What the lost-reason list suggests

The most recoverable groups in the uploaded list appear to be:

- Client is not ready yet
- No answer
- TM has no resources
- Found another contractor
- Budget too low / we are too expensive
- Project too small

These are better nurture targets than contacts marked:

- Not relevant anymore
- Hired internally
- Company was acquired
- Legal/security issues
- Interview or recruitment failure

## Start with the easiest topics

If the team already has an active nurture campaign, start with topics that are:

- broad enough to work across multiple segments
- easy to write without new research
- tied to timing, capacity, and delivery risk
- not too sales-heavy

### Recommended first 5 topics

1. **How to know when a Salesforce project is actually ready to start**
   - Best for: client not ready yet, no answer
   - Why this is easy: can be written as a short checklist
   - Angle: help buyers self-diagnose readiness instead of pushing for a call

2. **What to do when your team wants Salesforce work done but has no internal capacity**
   - Best for: TM has no resources
   - Why this is easy: very close to the core problem they already admitted
   - Angle: explain options like fractional support, phased delivery, and short-term specialist help

3. **3 low-risk Salesforce projects that create progress without a full transformation**
   - Best for: project too small, budget too low, client not ready yet
   - Why this is easy: list format, practical, low commitment
   - Angle: make the next step feel smaller and more affordable

4. **What to check before choosing a Salesforce partner**
   - Best for: found another contractor, still comparing vendors
   - Why this is easy: can be framed as buyer education, not a direct pitch
   - Angle: de-risk partner selection and reopen conversations if the current vendor underperforms

5. **How to rescue stalled Salesforce priorities without hiring a full team**
   - Best for: TM has no resources, no answer, client not ready yet
   - Why this is easy: combines urgency with a simple operational solution
   - Angle: speak to blocked teams that have demand but not bandwidth

## Best first topics to prioritize

If you only want to test a few topics first, use these in order:

1. **Project readiness checklist**
2. **No internal capacity / team bandwidth topic**
3. **Small, low-risk Salesforce wins**

Why these first:

- they match the biggest recoverable buckets
- they are easier to produce than deep case studies
- they fit both educational emails and soft CTA follow-ups
- they do not require heavy personalization

## Segment-to-topic map

### 1) Client is not ready yet

Use:

- readiness checklist
- signs it is the right time to start
- phased rollout plan
- low-risk first project ideas

Message angle:

"You do not need to launch a large engagement immediately. Here is how to tell whether now is the right time, and what a smaller first step can look like."

### 2) No answer

Use:

- short operational checklists
- "worth revisiting now?" style messages
- light industry proof or mini case examples

Message angle:

"Circling back with something useful rather than another generic follow-up."

For this group, the topic matters less than brevity. Keep email copy short and CTA light.

### 3) TM has no resources

Use:

- capacity gap content
- fractional or phased delivery support
- how to move forward without adding headcount

Message angle:

"If the priority is real but the bench is thin, here are practical ways to keep momentum without overcommitting."

### 4) Found another contractor

Use:

- how to evaluate Salesforce partners
- signs a project needs outside rescue support
- second-opinion or audit offer

Message angle:

"If your current route is working, great. If not, here is a simple way to validate delivery quality before more time is lost."

### 5) Budget too low / we are too expensive

Use:

- small-scope wins
- phased delivery
- prioritize highest-ROI backlog items first

Message angle:

"A smaller engagement can still unlock value if the scope is focused."

## Suggested 4-email test sequence

This is the simplest version to add into an active campaign.

### Email 1: Readiness

**Topic:** How to know if a Salesforce project is ready to start

**CTA:** Want the 5-point version tailored to your setup?

### Email 2: Capacity

**Topic:** What to do when the work matters but your team has no bandwidth

**CTA:** Reply with the blocked initiative and we will suggest a low-lift next step.

### Email 3: Small win

**Topic:** 3 Salesforce projects you can start without a large budget or long timeline

**CTA:** Want help choosing the best first project?

### Email 4: Partner choice / second opinion

**Topic:** What to check before committing to a Salesforce partner

**CTA:** Happy to share a quick evaluation framework.

## Keep the campaign simple

To maximize response rate, avoid starting with:

- heavy product pitches
- long-form thought leadership
- generic company news
- broad "checking in" emails with no value

Instead, use:

- checklists
- short frameworks
- "3 ways to..." formats
- phased approach messaging
- low-commitment CTAs

## Recommended next step

Launch the next nurture test with these three topics first:

1. **Are you ready to start?**
2. **How to move forward without internal bandwidth**
3. **What is the smallest Salesforce project worth doing first?**

That combination is the easiest to produce and the most likely to reconnect with lost SQLs that were delayed, unresponsive, or resource-constrained rather than truly closed-lost forever.
