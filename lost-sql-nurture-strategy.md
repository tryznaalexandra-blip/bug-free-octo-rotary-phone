# Lost SQL nurture strategy

## Goal

Use the existing active campaign to re-engage lost SQLs with the simplest topics most likely to restart a conversation, without building a heavily segmented program first.

## What the lost-reason data says

The uploaded list shows a few reasons dominating the pool:

- Client is not ready yet: 57
- Not relevant anymore: 46
- No answer: 37
- Found another contractor: 37
- TM has no resources: 36
- Budget too low: 18

This means the easiest win is not a deep persona-led nurture. It is a light reactivation track aimed at contacts who may still have a need, but timing, attention, or execution changed.

## Best starting segments

### 1. Start here: "timing slipped" segment

Use these reasons first:

- Client is not ready yet
- No answer

Why this is the easiest starting point:

- The deal was not necessarily lost on fit.
- The message can stay broad and useful.
- The CTA can be simple: reply with current priority or timing.
- These contacts fit naturally into an existing campaign without much new setup.

### 2. Test next: "still need help, but chose another path"

Use this reason second:

- Found another contractor

Why this is still workable:

- Some of these contacts may need backup support, specialist help, or a second option if the current partner underdelivers.
- The messaging can focus on low-risk ways to engage, not a full replacement sale.

### 3. Hold back for later

Do not prioritize these in the first pass:

- Not relevant anymore
- TM has no resources
- Budget too low
- TM has no expertise
- Project too small

Why to deprioritize them:

- They need tighter offer, pricing, or positioning changes.
- A generic nurture is less likely to move them.
- They add complexity before the simple reactivation motion is proven.

## Easy topics that might work

These topics are intentionally broad, practical, and low lift. They can be delivered as emails, linked articles, short PDFs, checklists, or repurposed case studies.

### Topic 1: "When is the right time to restart this project?"

Best for:

- Client is not ready yet
- No answer

Why it is easy:

- It matches the top loss reason directly.
- It does not require new product positioning.
- It invites a low-pressure response.

Content angle:

- Signs a Salesforce or RevOps project is ready to restart
- Common blockers that disappear after a quarter or budget cycle
- What to prepare before re-opening a project

CTA:

- "Is this back on the roadmap for this quarter or later this year?"

### Topic 2: "Quick wins before a full project"

Best for:

- Client is not ready yet
- No answer
- Budget-sensitive accounts later on

Why it is easy:

- It lowers perceived effort and risk.
- It keeps the conversation open even if the buyer is not ready for a big engagement.
- It can reuse existing audit, roadmap, or advisory offers.

Content angle:

- Small CRM or automation fixes that unlock value fast
- A short audit or advisory sprint instead of a full implementation
- How teams reduce risk before making a larger commitment

CTA:

- "Would a short audit or roadmap be more realistic than a full project right now?"

### Topic 3: "Extra capacity without hiring internally"

Best for:

- Found another contractor
- TM has no resources
- Hired internally or hiring internally

Why it is easy:

- It reframes the offer around flexibility, not replacement.
- It is relevant to internal bandwidth problems.
- It works even if the buyer already chose another option.

Content angle:

- Using external specialists for overflow or specialist gaps
- Bringing in support for one workstream instead of the full scope
- Fast help for admin, integration, QA, or delivery bottlenecks

CTA:

- "If your current team or partner needs overflow support, would a specialist bench be useful?"

### Topic 4: "What good looks like: simple customer examples"

Best for:

- All reactivation segments

Why it is easy:

- Case studies are usually easier to repurpose than net-new content.
- Social proof helps contacts who went quiet.
- It can be inserted into the current campaign without changing the structure.

Content angle:

- Before-and-after examples
- Short project stories with clear outcomes
- Practical lessons from similar teams

CTA:

- "Want me to send an example closest to your use case?"

## Recommended first campaign structure

Keep the first version simple:

1. Put **Client is not ready yet** and **No answer** into one reactivation stream.
2. Use **3-4 emails max** over a short cycle.
3. Reuse existing campaign assets wherever possible.
4. Ask for a light conversion, not a meeting right away.

Suggested sequence:

1. **Email 1 - Timing check-in**
   - Topic: restart timing
   - CTA: "Is this a priority this quarter?"
2. **Email 2 - Quick wins**
   - Topic: audit / advisory / low-lift engagement
   - CTA: "Would a small first step be more realistic?"
3. **Email 3 - Proof**
   - Topic: short case study
   - CTA: "Want the version most relevant to your team?"
4. **Email 4 - Breakup / status update**
   - Topic: simple permission-based close
   - CTA: "Should I circle back later, or can I close this out?"

## Recommended topic priority

If you want the shortest path to launch, start in this order:

1. **Restart timing / project readiness**
2. **Quick wins before a full project**
3. **Customer proof / mini case studies**
4. **Overflow capacity / specialist support**

Why this order:

- The first two directly address the biggest recoverable reasons.
- They require the least new creative work.
- They fit an existing campaign with minimal branching.

## Messaging guidance

Keep the tone:

- Helpful, not pushy
- Specific, not broad brand language
- Low-friction, with reply-based CTAs

Avoid:

- Heavy sales pressure
- Asking for a full discovery call in every touch
- Complex segmentation before the first test proves traction

## Success criteria for the first test

Track:

- Reply rate
- Positive reply rate
- Reactivated opportunities
- Reason updates from replies

The first goal is to learn which "lost" reasons are truly recyclable, not to maximize volume immediately.

## Clear recommendation

If we need to choose topics first and keep it easy, start with:

1. **Is now a better time to restart the project?**
2. **Could a smaller first step work instead of a full project?**
3. **Here is a simple example of the outcome another team got.**

That is the cleanest, lowest-effort nurture strategy for this lost SQL list and the best fit for an already active campaign.
